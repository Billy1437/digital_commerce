# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Myanmar (and some Thailand) mobile gamers, mostly on mid-range Android phones over patchy connections. They currently buy ML diamonds, PUBG UC and game accounts through Facebook groups, Viber chats and Telegram channels, where scams are common. Their job: top up or buy an account quickly, pay with a wallet they already have (KBZPay, Wave Money, PromptPay), and feel certain they won't be cheated.

## Product Purpose

GG Mart is a storefront that moves this gray-market trade onto a credible website: browse packages and accounts, order with QR payment, upload a slip, and a human admin verifies and delivers. Success = a first-time visitor trusts it enough to pay, and a repeat buyer can reorder in under a minute.

## Brand Personality

Modern gaming storefront, energetic but clean (user-chosen reference: "Gaming Galaxie" shot, June 2026). Dark charcoal surfaces, one signal-red accent carrying all CTAs and badges, white geometric sans type, image-led hero banner and cover-image product cards. Feels like a real game retailer (Epic/Steam-adjacent), not a chat-shop.

## Anti-references

- Scammy reseller aesthetics: stacked banners, blinking text, fake countdowns, sticker-bomb Facebook-shop clutter.
- Generic crypto-neon: purple gradients on black, glassmorphism everywhere, web3 template energy.
- Corporate SaaS: sterile white dashboards, stock illustrations, hero-metric stat rows.

## Design Principles

1. Calm is credibility: restraint, whitespace and composed motion signal legitimacy in a market full of noise.
2. Money looks like money: prices are always unmissable, formatted, and gold.
3. One orchestrated moment: a single staggered page-load reveal beats scattered effects.
4. Local, not translated: payment methods, Telegram, and Myanmar context are first-class, never bolted on.
5. The admin is the product: every surface reinforces that a real human verifies every order.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast on a dark theme, prefers-reduced-motion honored on all reveals, layouts that hold up on 360px-wide budget Android screens and slow networks (no heavy media, system fallbacks while fonts load).
